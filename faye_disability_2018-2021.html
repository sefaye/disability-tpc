2018–2021



While BPCQ published two special issues on disability in 2018, the number of articles on this topic in the journal falls immediately afterwards. There is only one article published in the 2018–2021 timeframe (Lorelei Ortiz, 2020) and only a few if we stretch our search to 2025 (Peter Cardon, 2025; Sherrie Drye et al., 2023; Alessandra Rossetti & Luke Van Waes, 2022). The topic does not completely drop off the map after the journal’s special issues, though, as the editor Melinda Knight kept attention on it through a series of editorials—lonely calls with little response. Similarly, CDQ offers one article (Krista Kennedy, 2018) published right after the special issue, while the rest of the articles on this topic pick up again in 2024.

Outside of the 2018 BPCQ and CDQ special issues, and in between these special issues and IEEE’s 2021 special issue, a handful of articles focus on disability in a variety of TPC journals. The topics in these articles vary considerably. To name just a few: lack of attention to embodiment (Mary Clinkenbeard 2020; Caleb Milligan, 2019; Kathryn Swacha, 2018); neurodiversity as gain for the workplace (Lorelei Ortiz, 2020); disability studies theory as a gain for TPC (Zsuzsanna Palmer et al., 2021); making maps accessible to people who are visually impaired (Megan Conway et al., 2020); making courses accessible (Jessie Borgman & Jason Dockter, 2018; Stephanie Vie, 2018); disability activism (Talea Anderson, 2021); inclusive usability testing (Liz Hutter & Halcyon Lawrence, 2018); and the presence of ableist language in mental health documents (Peter Cannon & Katie Walkup, 2021). As the number of subtopics on disability increases, the ramifications of this topic are clear: It is everywhere, so TPCers cannot ignore it anymore. The IEEE 2021 special issue reinforces this commitment to the topic of disability by highlighting its cruciality for the field.
