Searching for the Aging Body in Technical and Professional Communication

While I was locating articles on disability in the main TPC publications, I simultaneously collected articles focusing on older adults (some overlapping the two topics).

During my search, I was haunted by this question: Where is the scholarship on older adults (age 70+) in TPC? Searching through all the main publications in TPC, I only found 18 articles focusing on older adults.

1990s

  • “Media Design” (O.L. Hill, 1995; TC)
  • “User Instructions” (Maartje Van Hees, 1996; Journal of Technical Writing and Communication)

2000s

  • “Manuals for the Elderly” (F.M. Van Horen et al., 2001; Journal of Technical Writing and Communication)
  • “Computers and Aging” (Angela Crow, 2002; Journal of Technical Writing and Communication)
  • “Effects of Pictures” (Hans Van Der Meij & Mark Gellevij, 2002; TC)
  • “A Generational Approach” (Franklin Krohn, 2004; Journal of Technical Writing and Communication)
  • “Gray Matters” (Gail Lippincott, 2004; IEEE)
  • “Curb Cuts” (Karen O’Hara, 2004; Technical Communication Quarterly)
  • “New Heuristics” (Dana Chisnell et al., 2006; TC)
  • “Conducting Usability” (Thea van Der Geest, 2006; TC)
  • “Adding Motivation” (Nicole Loorbach et al., 2007; TC)

2010s

  • “Advocates in a Self-Service Society” (Yvonne Cleary & Madelyn Flammia, 2012; Journal of Technical Writing and Communication)
  • “Confidence Increasing” (Nicole Loorbach et al., 2013; TC)
  • “Icon Design” (Ljilja Kascak et al., 2013; CDQ)
  • “Bridging the Gap” (Kathryn Swacha, 2018; Technical Communication Quarterly)

2020s

  • “mHealth” (Russell Kirkscey, 2020; Journal of Technical Writing and Communication)
  • “Everyone is Always Aging” (Allegra Smith, 2022; TC)
  • “Twilight Zone” (Rachel Tofteland-Trampe, 2025; CDQ)

Trends

Among these 18 articles, we find recurring trends:

Trend 1

Use of previously published literature to learn about this population group rather than direct communication with older users (Cleary & Flammia, 2012; Hill, 1995; Krohn, 2004; Chisnell et al., 2006; Van Hees, 1996). This secondhand knowledge could create a reliance on the same stereotypes, depicting older adults as homogeneous and as a deficit population through a hyperfocus on their ability losses.

Trend 2

Usability tests involving older adults but with a lack of attention to the embodied experiences of these users (Kascak et al., 2013; Kirkscey, 2020; Van Der Geest, 2006; Van Der Meij & Gellevij, 2002; Van Horen et al., 2001). Some scholars performed the tests in the participants’ homes and included quotes from them in their findings, but there is very little description of users’ embodied experiences (Loorbach et al., 2007; 2013). Such omission risks overfocusing on the task rather than the users and potentially disregarding the user’s expertise or perspective.

Trend 3

A mostly recent, positive move in TPC research towards careful attention to the aging body and the lives of the older adults (Crow, 2002; O’Hara, 2004; Smith, 2022; Swacha, 2018; Tofteland-Trampe, 2025).

  • The authors focused on specific individuals and went into detail on their complex lives.
  • Their descriptions of the older adults were positive.
  • They interacted deeply with their participants, treating them as experts.

A Call to Action

Lippincott (2004) scolded the field for not researching older adults, despite the graying of the population, the increased use of technology by older adults, and the potential gains for our scholarship in learning more about our wide variety of users. All the articles published after Lippincott (2004) cited this article, but not all necessarily followed its recommendations.

Takeaways

First, despite the obvious point that our field needs to increase its interest in older adults and conduct more research on this population, we also find a discrepancy between TPC’s complex theories of embodiment (Mary Clinkenbeard, 2020; Erin Frost, 2018; Lisa Melonçon, 2013, 2018; Swacha, 2018) and not enough application of them towards older users in our research projects.

However, the main takeaway is that this population remains understudied and misrepresented. This does not bode well for TPC’s social justice goals of giving voice to marginalized users and adapting technical communication to their needs and embodied experiences.
