Disability and Technical and Professional Communication: A Journey Through Six Special Issues

by Sarah Faye

How This Project Started



In 2021, IEEE Transactions on Professional Communication (IEEE) published a special issue on accessibility. I felt galvanized reading Lisa Melonçon and Nupoor Ranade’s (2021) bold introduction that calls for a paradigm shift to center accessibility in technical and professional communication (TPC), to truly advocate for people with disabilities. However, as I went back in time and read previous TPC articles on disability, I realized there was nothing new in the idea of the paradigm shift. Scholars in TPC have called for this shift, in one form or another, for the past 35+ years.

In the main TPC journals, there are six special issues dedicated to disability and accessibility, appearing before and after the passage of the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) in 1990 and providing an insightful illustration of the shifts in how this topic has been covered in TPC scholarship. These six special issues showed that there has been an interest in the field for this topic, but the repetition of the same calls throughout the issues also highlights some disciplinary inertia to change when it comes to disability.

In fact, what piqued my interest in exploring this topic in TPC publications was the echoing of the same critique in our field for our lackadaisical commitment to this topic. No matter where I searched, the topic of disability was always accompanied by serious complaints on how we discuss and research it in TPC.

The scholars merging disability studies with TPC call out the field for not researching the topic of disability enough (Leah Heilig, 2023; Melinda Knight, 2018; Melonçon, 2013; Sushil Oswal & Melonçon, 2014) and for holding on to simplistic thinking about disability that still shapes our scholarship and ableist practices. They argue that TPC scholars are often

  • using outdated theories on disability (Kristin Bennett, 2023; Shannon Walters, 2010),
  • excluding people with disabilities from their research (Oswal & Zsuzsanna Palmer, 2021),
  • thinking of users in normative terms and ignoring complex embodied experiences, (Mary Clinkenbeard, 2020; Erin Frost, 2018; Lichen Hamraie, 2017, 2021; Melonçon, 2013; Oswal, 2023),
  • using normative language (Bennett, 2023; Jason Palmeri, 2006),
  • trying to “fix” disability through technology (Hamraie, 2023), and
  • retrofitting accommodations instead of centering TPC work on accessibility from the outset (Jay Dolmage, 2008; Heilig, 2023; Melonçon & Ranade, 2021; Oswal, 2023; Sean Zdenek, 2018a; 2020).

In particular, editors of special issues on disability and accessibility and other TPC experts on this topic have repeated ad nauseam the call for the paradigm shift from 1989 to 2025 (to name just a few: Jodie Gilmore, 1989; Meredith Johnson, & Lauren Cagle, 2025; Melonçon & Ranade, 2021; Oswal, 2018; Theofanos & Redish, 2005; Willers, 2024; Zdenek, 2018a). The term “paradigm shift” (p. 17) originated with Mary Frances Theofanos and Janice (Ginny) Redish (2005), who called for including disabled users in our work and centering accessibility in all TPC projects and research rather than thinking of it as retrofit.

Curious about the paradox between the special issues highlighting the importance of this topic and the field’s overall lack of response, I decided to follow the topic of disability throughout the publication history of the main journals in TPC, focusing in particular on the six special issues dedicated to accessibility within these four publications:

  • Communication Design Quarterly (CDQ), 2018 (6.4)
  • Business and Professional Communication Quarterly (BPCQ), 2018 (81.1) and (81.4)
  • IEEE Transactions on Professional Communication (IEEE), 1985 (28.4) and 2021 (64.3)
  • Technical Communication (TC), 2006 (53.1)

To situate the special issues in their disciplinary and historical context, I also collected all articles focusing on disability and accessibility in these four journals, as well as all articles focusing on this topic within the entire publication history until 2025 of the following additional journals (more detailed information can be found in the methods page):

  • Computers and Composition
  • Journal of Business and Technical Communication
  • Journal of Technical Writing and Communication
  • Kairos
  • Technical Communication Quarterly

The general research question that guided me during this project was: How has TPC journal coverage of disability changed over time, given the passionate calls for change and the limited responses to those calls?

Of course, within this very large question were nested other, more specific questions:

  • How are TPCers understanding the connections between accessibility and technology?
  • What is the role of the TPC classroom when it comes to training students for accessibility?
  • What do the ignored, repeated calls reveal about the values and sense of identity of TPC?

I situate my project within the scholarly debates merging disability and TPC, namely the tensions between the calls to understand disability as a gain for our field and some enduring ableist tendencies of ignoring people with disabilities as well as a lingering lack of true disciplinary commitment to accessibility.

This web-text focuses on the special issues, but I also performed deep dives on, among other things, game-changing articles, editorials calling for more research on this topic, and the shifts in how the field tackled the topic of disability in-between the special issues. While the main pages of this web-text focus on the special issues and follow them chronologically, the embedded links within each special issue page give you an opportunity to plunge into the transitional deep dives, my methods, and additional information on concepts and theories.
