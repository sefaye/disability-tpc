Kairos 2002 Special Issue

In 2002, Kairos published a special issue on disability and technology. While all the authors’ explorations of disability went deeper in complexity than those found in the TC 2006 special issue, I have not included this special issue on disability in my set of special issues because the main focus of the articles is not technical communication. For that same reason, I have not included Kairos 2013 special issue (18.1, Accessibility and Multimodal Composition): both M. Remi Yergeau et al (2013)’s article and Tara Wood and Shannon Madden (2013)’s article are not focused on technical communication.

However, the 2002 articles are remarkable for their early explorations of disability studies and their arguments that accessibility helps everyone.

A few of these articles focus on the intersections of disability and writing courses. Patricia Dunn and Kathleen Dunn De Mers (2002) argued that universal design helps everyone in our classes, providing helpful descriptions and examples of this design practice. Brenda Brueggemann et al. (2002) shared the experience and reflection of multiple participants in the course “Disability in Drama and Performance Art.” Finally, Susan Grover and Anne Hendricks (2002) discussed accommodations for students with learning disabilities and argue that those accommodations benefit everyone.

We also have a focus on Deaf culture in two articles. Michael Salvo (2002) reported on the frustration and anger of Deaf students during an unsatisfying process of requesting accommodations from their institution. Marlana Portolano (2002) described her experience adopting a Deaf child, sharing introductory information on ASL, Deaf culture, the Cochlear implant, and practical information for other adaptive parents, concluding with reflections on the insights this experience gave her on teaching English.

The articles are followed by two conversations with several authors from this special issue to discuss creating web-texts about disability and different communications strategies for those who are hearing impaired. While most other articles on disability of the early 2000s were focused on website accessibility, those in the Kairos 2002 special issue follow a more complex understanding of disability, following the direction set by James Wilson’s (2000) article: a heightened awareness of the complexity of the topic and a strengthening of the connection between disability and the overall field of Writing Studies.
